You need more space. The question is: do you extend your current home or move to a bigger one?

This decision involves emotion, lifestyle, schools, community — things that can't be reduced to numbers. But the financial component can be calculated. And getting the numbers right stops you making a decision you'll regret.

## The Framework

The decision comes down to a comparison:

**Option A: Extend**
Total cost of extension + value of current home after extension

**Option B: Move**
Total cost of moving + value of new home

If Option A leaves you with more equity (or equal equity with less disruption), extending wins. If Option B gives you a better home for similar total outlay, moving wins.

Let's break down each side.

## Calculating the True Cost to Extend

Most people underestimate this significantly.

<Callout type="important">
The builder's quote is not the cost of extending. It's one component of a larger total.
</Callout>

### Direct Building Costs
- Builder's quote
- Professional fees (architect, structural engineer)
- Building control and planning fees
- Party wall costs
- VAT (if applicable)

### Associated Costs
- Temporary accommodation during build
- Storage for furniture
- Increased insurance during works
- Landscaping reinstatement
- New furniture for additional space

### Finance Costs
- Interest on additional borrowing
- Arrangement fees for new/extended mortgage
- Valuation fees

### Hidden Costs
- Your time managing the project
- Stress and disruption (real, even if hard to quantify)
- Risk of cost overruns (add 15-20% contingency)

<ExampleBox title="Example Extension Cost">
Quoted building work: £60,000

Add:
- Professional fees: £6,000
- Party wall: £1,500
- Building control: £800
- Temporary accommodation (3 months): £4,500
- Landscaping: £3,000
- Furniture: £2,000
- Contingency (15%): £11,700

**True extension cost: £89,500**
</ExampleBox>

## Calculating the True Cost to Move

People underestimate this too, but in different ways.

### Transaction Costs
- Stamp duty on new property
- Estate agent fees on sale (1-3% + VAT)
- Legal fees for sale
- Legal fees for purchase
- Mortgage arrangement fees

### Moving Costs
- Removals company
- Storage (if needed between moves)
- Temporary accommodation (if chain breaks)

### New Property Costs
- Any renovation or decoration needed
- New furniture or appliances
- Garden setup

<ExampleBox title="Example Moving Cost">
Current home value: £450,000
Target home value: £600,000

Stamp duty (on £600k): £20,000
Estate agent (1.5% + VAT of £450k): £8,100
Legal fees (sale + purchase): £3,500
Mortgage fees: £1,000
Removals: £1,500
Decoration allowance: £5,000

**True moving cost: £39,100**
**Additional property cost: £150,000**
**Total new investment: £189,100**
</ExampleBox>

## The Comparison

Now compare:

<Stat value="£89,500" label="to extend vs £189,100 to move (example)" />

In the example above, extending costs £100,000 less than moving. But that's not the whole picture.

### Value Added by Extension

Extensions don't add pound-for-pound value. A £90,000 extension might add £50,000-70,000 to your property's value. The rest is the "cost of living" — you pay a premium for the space, but you don't get it back when you sell.

### What You Get for Moving

The extra £100,000 in the move scenario gets you a different house. Possibly bigger. Possibly in better condition. Possibly with a garden that doesn't need rebuilding after construction.

### The Breakeven Analysis

Work out what your home would be worth after the extension. Compare that to what you'd own after moving.

**After extending:**
- Current home value: £450,000
- Extension value added: £60,000 (optimistic)
- Remaining mortgage: £200,000
- **Equity: £310,000**

**After moving:**
- New home value: £600,000
- New mortgage: £389,100 (old mortgage + additional investment)
- **Equity: £210,900**

In this example, extending leaves you with more equity. But you're comparing a £510,000 property against a £600,000 property. They're not equivalent outcomes.

## When Extending Wins

Extending usually makes more sense when:

- Your location is exceptional and equivalent houses nearby cost significantly more
- The extension is relatively modest (single storey, not affecting the whole house)
- You have strong emotional attachment to the home
- Children are in crucial school years
- The neighbourhood is appreciating faster than the wider market
- You have specific needs that are cheaper to build than to find (e.g., accessibility adaptations)

## When Moving Wins

Moving usually makes more sense when:

- You need significantly more space (doubling bedrooms, for example)
- Your current home has fundamental limitations (ceiling heights, orientation, garden size)
- The property market is flat or falling (stamp duty becomes smaller relative to values)
- You'd need to move eventually anyway (growing family, different life stage)
- Your current location has drawbacks you'd like to escape
- Equivalent extended homes sell for prices that reveal extensions don't add much value locally

## The Non-Financial Factors

Numbers matter, but they're not everything.

### Disruption
- Extending: 3-12 months of living in a building site (or temporary accommodation)
- Moving: 2-3 months of stress, then it's done

### Risk
- Extending: Cost overruns, builder disputes, planning issues, design problems
- Moving: Chain collapse, survey surprises, timing issues

### Certainty
- Extending: You don't fully know what you're getting until it's built
- Moving: You can see exactly what you're buying

### Community
- Extending: You keep your neighbours, your school run, your commute
- Moving: Everything changes

<Callout type="tip">
If the numbers are close (within 20% either way), let the non-financial factors decide. Both options are financially defensible — choose based on what matters to your life.
</Callout>

## Making the Decision

1. **Calculate the true cost of extending** — every category, with contingency
2. **Calculate the true cost of moving** — including stamp duty and all transaction costs
3. **Estimate value added by extension** — be conservative
4. **Compare final equity positions** — what do you own after each option?
5. **Compare what you get** — is the extended home equivalent to the target move home?
6. **Factor in non-financial considerations** — disruption, risk, community
7. **Make the decision** — and commit to it fully
