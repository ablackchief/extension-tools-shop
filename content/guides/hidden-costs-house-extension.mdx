You've got a builder's quote. The number looks manageable. You've done your sums, spoken to the bank, and the project seems affordable.

Then you start the build. And the invoices start arriving from people you've never heard of, for things you didn't know existed.

<Stat value="30%" label="average budget overrun due to 'forgotten' costs" />

This is the hidden cost problem. Not hidden because anyone's being dishonest — hidden because the building quote only covers building work. Everything else is extra.

Here's the complete list.

## Professional Fees

### 1. Architect or Designer Fees

Unless you're building something very simple, you'll need drawings. These aren't free.

- **Simple extension**: £2,000-4,000
- **Complex extension**: £5,000-12,000
- **Full architectural service**: 8-12% of construction cost

<Callout type="tip">
Get a fixed fee quote rather than a percentage. It removes the perverse incentive for costs to rise.
</Callout>

### 2. Structural Engineer

Your architect's drawings show what it looks like. The structural engineer's calculations prove it won't fall down. Essential for any extension with:
- Openings in load-bearing walls
- Steelwork
- Foundations
- Alterations to the roof structure

**Cost: £500-2,000** depending on complexity.

### 3. Party Wall Surveyor

If you're building on or near the boundary with a neighbour, the Party Wall Act applies. You need a surveyor. If your neighbour appoints their own surveyor, you pay for both.

**Cost: £700-5,000** depending on complexity and neighbour cooperation.

### 4. Building Control Fees

Your extension needs Building Regulations approval. The council (or a private inspector) charges for this.

**Cost: £400-1,200** depending on project size.

### 5. Planning Application Fee

If you need planning permission (not all extensions do), there's a fee.

**Cost: £258** for a householder application (2024 rates, rises periodically).

## Survey Costs

### 6. Measured Survey

Before design starts, someone needs to measure your existing house accurately. Don't assume your architect includes this.

**Cost: £300-800**

### 7. Drainage Survey (CCTV)

If your extension goes over existing drains, you need to know where they are and what condition they're in.

**Cost: £200-400**

### 8. Structural Survey

For older properties, a structural survey before work starts can identify issues that would become expensive surprises later.

**Cost: £400-800**

### 9. Asbestos Survey

Pre-1999 buildings may contain asbestos in unexpected places (artex ceilings, floor tiles, pipe insulation). Testing and potential removal is a legal requirement.

**Cost: £200-500** for survey; removal can be £500-5,000+

## Site Costs

### 10. Skip Hire and Waste Removal

The builder's quote might include waste from their work. It probably doesn't include clearing the site beforehand or disposing of items from inside your house.

**Cost: £300-600** per skip; multiple skips often needed.

### 11. Scaffolding

If your extension is more than single-storey or requires work at height, scaffolding is essential.

**Cost: £500-2,000** depending on duration and extent.

### 12. Temporary Accommodation

If the build makes your home unliveable (no kitchen, no bathroom, structural instability), you may need to move out.

**Cost: £1,000-3,000/month** for rental; or reduced living quality.

### 13. Storage

Your furniture needs to go somewhere while walls are being knocked down.

**Cost: £100-300/month**

## Utility Costs

### 14. Electricity Supply Upgrade

Older houses often have outdated consumer units. A new extension may require upgrading the incoming supply or the distribution board.

**Cost: £500-2,000**

### 15. Gas Meter Relocation

If the meter is where the extension will be built, it needs to move first. This is a regulated process with long lead times.

**Cost: £500-1,500**

### 16. Water Connection

New bathrooms or kitchens need water supply. If the existing supply is inadequate, upgrading involves groundwork and utility company fees.

**Cost: £500-2,500**

### 17. Drainage Connection

New waste water needs to go somewhere. Connecting to existing drains may require building over agreements or new connections.

**Cost: £500-3,000**

<Callout type="warning">
Utility work often has the longest lead times. Start these enquiries first, even before your builder's quote is finalised.
</Callout>

## Finishing Costs

### 18. Kitchen or Bathroom Fitting

The builder's quote covers the structure. Kitchens and bathrooms are usually supply and fit by specialists.

**Cost: £5,000-30,000+** depending on specification.

### 19. Flooring

Unless specifically itemised, flooring is often provisional or excluded.

**Cost: £30-150/sqm** depending on material.

### 20. Decoration

The builder may include a mist coat, but full decoration (painting, wallpapering) is often extra.

**Cost: £500-3,000** depending on whether you DIY.

### 21. Landscaping

Your garden will be destroyed by the build. Reinstating it costs money.

**Cost: £1,000-10,000** depending on scope.

## Administrative Costs

### 22. Insurance Adjustments

Your home insurance needs updating during and after the build. Construction phase requires specialist cover.

**Cost: £200-500** for construction period insurance; increased premiums ongoing.

### 23. Mortgage Fees

If you're borrowing against the property, there may be arrangement fees, valuation fees, or early repayment penalties on existing deals.

**Cost: £0-3,000** depending on your situation.

## The Real Budget

<ExampleBox title="Example: Rear Extension True Cost">
Builder's quote: £45,000

Add:
- Architect: £3,500
- Structural engineer: £800
- Party wall surveyor: £1,200
- Building control: £600
- Drainage survey: £300
- Skip hire: £1,200
- Scaffolding: £800
- Kitchen: £12,000
- Flooring: £2,000
- Decoration: £1,500
- Landscaping: £2,500
- Insurance: £300
- Contingency (10%): £7,000

**Actual budget needed: £78,700**
</ExampleBox>

That's 75% more than the builder's quote. Not because the builder was dishonest — because the builder only prices building work.

## How to Protect Yourself

1. **Use a comprehensive budget template** that forces you to consider every category
2. **Get quotes for professional fees before committing** to the project
3. **Add 20-30% contingency** for unknowns
4. **Don't start until financing is confirmed** for the true total, not just the building quote
